### About the Experiment

The manufacturing of semiconductor chips requires precise modification of the silicon substrate's electrical properties to create active components like transistors. Once the baseline wafer surfaces are prepared, it goes through an ion implantation process wherein high-velocity dopant atoms are physically accelerated and blasted directly into the target regions of the silicon crystal lattice. This changes the localized substrate chemistry, creating the distinct P-type or N-type well formations needed to guide electrical current.There are two primary doping profiles engineered during this phase: N-Type regions (using Phosphorus or Arsenic ions) which introduce an abundance of free electrons, and P-Type regions (using Boron ions) which introduce positive charge carriers known as holes.Through this experiment, students can simulate these distinct dopant impurity profiles along with other parametric changes—such as Implantation Acceleration Energy, Ion Target Dosage, and Exposure Time—to analyze the final outputs in terms of junction depth ($R_p$), sheet resistance ($R_s$), and implant uniformity.
