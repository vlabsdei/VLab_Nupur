Thermal oxidation is an important semiconductor fabrication process used to grow a thin silicon dioxide (SiO₂) layer on the surface of a silicon wafer. This oxide layer acts as an insulating and protective coating during semiconductor device manufacturing.

In this process, silicon wafers are heated at high temperatures between **800°C and 1200°C** in the presence of oxygen or water vapor. The oxygen reacts with silicon to form a uniform **SiO₂** layer on the wafer surface.

The thermal oxidation process is widely used in IC fabrication because the oxide layer:

- Prevents electrical leakage
- Protects the wafer during processing
- Acts as an insulating layer in MOS devices


# Types of Thermal Oxidation

## Dry Oxidation

- Uses pure oxygen gas (**O₂**)
- Produces thin and high-quality oxide layers
- Growth rate is relatively slow
- Commonly used for gate oxide formation

## Wet Oxidation

- Uses oxygen and water vapor (**H₂O**)
- Produces thicker oxide layers rapidly
- Oxide density is comparatively lower
- Used when thick oxide layers are required

# Factors Affecting Oxide Growth

The oxide thickness mainly depends on:

- Oxidation temperature
- Heating time
- Oxidation type (dry or wet)

Higher temperature and longer oxidation time increase oxide growth rate.

# Growth Rate Formula

The average oxide growth rate is calculated as:

Growth Rate = Oxide Thickness / Oxidation Time
