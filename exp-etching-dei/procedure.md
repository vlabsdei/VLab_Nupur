### Procedure
1. Select the desired etching branch on the settings panel using the **Etch Mechanism** dropdown menu:
   * **Wet Chemical Isotropic Etching**
   * **Dry Reactive Ion Anisotropic Etching (RIE)**

2. Click on the **Patterned Wafer** icon card within the equipment tray to load the substrate.

3. Observe the cross-sectional view of the wafer initialized on the central **Wafer Stage**, showing the Photoresist layer protecting the structural fields of the SiO2 layer below.

4. Set the operational process parameters using the sliders within the allowed operating ranges:
   * Acid Concentration (Wet Etch): **10 – 80 %**
   * RF Plasma Power (Dry Etch): **50 – 300 W**
   * Etch Process Time: **10 – 60 sec**

5. For optimized structure profile output conditions, maintain approximately:
   * Acid Concentration (Wet Etch): **30 – 50 %**
   * RF Plasma Power (Dry Etch): **100 – 200 W**
   * Etch Process Time: **25 – 45 sec**

6. Click the **Fill Wet Bench** button (for Wet Mode) or **Pump Gas / Ignite** button (for Dry Mode) to prep the respective machine housing environment.

7. Observe the telemetry feedback systems update (fluid level height rising or violet reactive plasma glow discharge initiating inside the vacuum chamber ring).

8. Click the **Initiate Etch** button to bring the surface interfaces into contact with the reactive agents and begin material layer removal.

9. Observe the structural cross-section carefully and monitor:
   * Under-digging profile propagation
   * Instantaneous chemical removal rate velocity
   * Calculated final device yield quality percentage
   * Active chemical/plasma process tracking hazard warnings

10. Click the **Strip Photoresist** button to dissolve away the dark protective polymer masks and expose the finalized micro-structures.

11. Shift parameter configurations and repeat the experiment steps to evaluate varied cross-sectional failure/success behaviors.


### Observations

1. Wet chemical processing removes target thin-film material symmetrically in all directions, yielding an isotropic undercut profile beneath mask edges.

2. Reactive Ion Plasma (RIE) shielding structures utilize vertical ion flux directionality to produce clean anisotropic vertical side-walls.

3. Running wet chemistry loops under high chemical concentration values speeds up processing but sharply reduces profile selectivity tracking indices.

4. Over-extending process operational time window constraints beyond limits induces mask over-digging threshold layer structural failures.

5. Device layer final yield quality decreases when parameter profiles deviate away from stable target windows.


### Result

The Semiconductor Thin-Film Etching processing sequence was successfully simulated, and the direct mechanical influence of chemical type choices, parameter concentration scales, plasma power wattages, and runtime exposures on final micro-structure profiles was analyzed.
