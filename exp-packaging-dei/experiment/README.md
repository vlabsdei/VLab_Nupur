### About the Experiment

The manufacturing of semiconductor chips concludes with safeguarding the fragile, functional silicon pieces after they are tested and cut from the wafer. Once the individual chip dies are verified, they go through a packaging and assembly process wherein the silicon is mounted onto a carrier and sealed inside a rugged frame. This keeps the delicate logic circuitry completely insulated from moisture and protected from physical shocks.

There are two primary styles of connecting the chip's internal wiring to the outside world: Thermosonic Ball Bonding, which uses heat and acoustic waves to weld gold or copper wires, and Wedge Bonding, which uses ultrasonic friction to flatten aluminum wire contacts.

Through this experiment, students can simulate these interconnect styles along with other parametric changes such as Capillary Ultrasonic Force and Encapsulation Molding Temperature to observe the final outputs in terms of bond pull strength, internal wire sweep rates, delamination scores, and overall package structural reliability.
