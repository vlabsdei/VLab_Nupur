### About the Experiment

The thin-film deposition process is a fundamental semiconductor fabrication step used to grow ultra-thin layers of insulating, conducting, or semiconducting materials onto a silicon substrate. These microscopic layers, ranging from a few nanometers to several micrometers in thickness, form the structural features, wiring interconnects, and protective barriers of integrated circuits.

This experiment helps students understand the high-vacuum processing setup of both Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) and Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD) techniques. By adjusting critical manufacturing parameters such as deposition rate, processing time, and chemical precursor materials users can directly analyze how growth kinetics dictate final film thickness, uniformity, step coverage quality, and overall device performance.
