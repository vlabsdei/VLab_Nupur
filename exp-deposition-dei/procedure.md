### Procedure
1. Select the desired deposition technique on the settings panel using the **Deposition Type** dropdown menu:

   * **Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)**
   * **Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD)**

2. Click on the **Silicon Wafer** icon within the equipment tray to load the substrate into the deposition chamber.

3. Observe the wafer positioned on the central **Wafer Chuck** inside the vacuum deposition chamber, ready for thin-film growth.

4. Choose the desired thin-film material using the **Material** dropdown menu:

   * **Silicon Dioxide (SiO₂)**
   * **Polysilicon**
   * **Aluminum**

5. Set the process parameters using the sliders within the specified operating ranges:

   * Deposition Rate: **5 – 100 nm/min**
   * Deposition Time: **1 – 30 min**

6. For optimum thin-film quality, maintain approximately:

   * Deposition Rate: **30 – 70 nm/min**
   * Deposition Time: **8 – 15 min**

7. Click the **Prepare Source** button to initialize the deposition system.

   * For **CVD**, the gas delivery showerhead is activated.
   * For **PVD**, the sputtering target is prepared and the vacuum chamber is configured.

8. Click the **Start Deposition** button to initiate thin-film deposition.

9. Observe the process throughout the deposition cycle and monitor:

   * Gas molecules or sputtered atoms travelling toward the wafer.
   * Plasma glow formation during PVD operation.
   * Layer-by-layer thin-film growth on the wafer surface.
   * Live countdown timer indicating the remaining deposition duration.
   * Continuous increase in calculated film thickness.

10. After completion of the process, examine the generated output parameters:

* Final Film Thickness
* Surface Coverage
* Layer Quality
* Process Status

11. Modify the deposition technique, material selection, deposition rate, or deposition time and repeat the experiment to compare the influence of different operating conditions on thin-film growth characteristics.

### Observations

1. Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) produces highly conformal thin films with excellent surface and step coverage due to chemical reactions occurring uniformly over the wafer.

2. Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD) deposits material primarily in a directional manner, making it suitable for metallic thin-film formation but with comparatively lower step coverage.

3. Increasing the deposition rate increases the film growth rate, resulting in thicker deposited layers within the same process duration.

4. Increasing deposition time directly increases the final film thickness according to the relation:

   **Film Thickness = Deposition Rate × Deposition Time**

5. Maintaining deposition parameters within the recommended operating range results in improved film uniformity and higher layer quality, while operating outside these limits may reduce deposition quality.

### Result

The Thin Film Deposition process was successfully simulated using both Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) and Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD) techniques. The effect of deposition method, material selection, deposition rate, and deposition time on film thickness, surface coverage, and layer quality was observed and analyzed successfully.
