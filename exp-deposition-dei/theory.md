## THEORY
### Introduction

Deposition is a semiconductor fabrication process in which thin layers of material are added onto the surface of a silicon wafer. These deposited layers are used to form insulating layers, conducting layers, and protective coatings required for integrated circuit fabrication.

Unlike etching, which removes material, deposition adds material to the wafer surface.

Common deposited materials include:

- Silicon Dioxide (SiO₂)
- Silicon Nitride (Si₃N₄)
- Polysilicon
- Metal Films (Aluminum, Copper, Tungsten)

## Types of Deposition

### Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)

In Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD), gaseous chemicals react near the wafer surface and form a thin solid film.

**Features:**

- Good film uniformity
- Excellent surface coverage
- Widely used in semiconductor manufacturing


### Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD)

In Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD), material is physically ejected from a source and deposited onto the wafer surface.

**Features:**

- Faster deposition process
- Commonly used for metal layers
- Includes sputtering and evaporation techniques

## Factors Affecting Deposition

### Deposition Rate

The deposition rate determines how quickly material is deposited onto the wafer. Higher deposition rates produce thicker films in less time.

### Deposition Time

The longer the deposition process continues, the thicker the deposited layer becomes.

### Material Type

Different materials have different deposition characteristics and are used for different purposes in semiconductor devices.


## Deposition Thickness Relationship

The thickness of the deposited layer can be estimated using:

### Formula

\[
\text{Thickness} = \text{Deposition Rate} \times \text{Deposition Time}
\]

Where:

- **Thickness** = Deposited film thickness (nm)
- **Deposition Rate** = Growth rate of the material (nm/min)
- **Deposition Time** = Duration of deposition (min)


## Importance of Deposition

Deposition is a critical step in semiconductor manufacturing because it enables the formation of multiple functional layers on a wafer. These layers are used to build transistors, interconnections, insulating regions, and protective coatings.
Modern integrated circuits contain many deposited layers stacked on top of one another to create complex electronic devices.
