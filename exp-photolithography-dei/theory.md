# Theory

Photolithography is one of the most important semiconductor manufacturing processes used to create microscopic circuit patterns on silicon wafers. These patterns later form transistors, interconnections, and other electronic components inside integrated circuits (ICs).

In this process, a light-sensitive material called **photoresist** is first coated onto the wafer surface using a high-speed spinning process called **spin coating**. The thickness of the photoresist layer depends on the spin speed. Higher spin speed produces a thinner coating.

After coating, a patterned mask is placed above the wafer. Ultraviolet (UV) light is then passed through the mask. The exposed regions of the photoresist undergo chemical changes.

The wafer is then developed using a chemical developer solution. Depending on the type of photoresist used, either the exposed or unexposed regions are removed, leaving behind the required circuit pattern on the wafer surface.

The patterned photoresist layer acts as a protective mask during later semiconductor processes such as etching, doping, and material deposition.


# Types of Photoresist

## Positive Photoresist

* Exposed regions become soluble
* Exposed portions are removed during development
* Produces finer and high-resolution patterns

## Negative Photoresist

* Exposed regions become hard
* Unexposed portions are removed
* Used for thicker and specialized structures

# Factors Affecting Photolithography

The quality of pattern formation depends on:

* Spin speed
* UV exposure time
* Mask alignment
* Photoresist thickness

Improper parameter selection may lead to:

* incomplete patterns
* distorted structures
* poor resolution
* alignment defects


# Formula Used

## Photoresist Thickness Relation
t_PR ∝ 1 / √ω

Where:

* (t_{PR}) = photoresist thickness
* (ω) = spin speed

This means:

* Higher spin speed → thinner coating
* Lower spin speed → thicker coating
