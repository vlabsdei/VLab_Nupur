### About the Experiment

The manufacturing of semiconductor chips relies heavily on precise micro-patterning transfer techniques to build miniature circuit components. And once the protective silicon dioxide layer is safely grown on the wafer, this substrate goes through a photolithography process wherein a light-sensitive polymer coating is applied, aligned, and exposed to replicate complex circuit layouts. This defines the exact windows and pathways needed for subsequent chip fabrication steps.

There are two main phases of parameters that dictate the resolution—Spin Speed and Optical Exposure Time.

Through this experiment, students can simulate both the chemical coating and radiation exposure types along with other parametric changes such as Temperature and Time and get the output in terms of the photoresist film thickness and pattern quality resolution.
